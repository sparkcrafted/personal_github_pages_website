Warren A. Jones II is a strategic operations leader whose work sits at the intersection of nonprofit operations, governance, data, and decision-making. His work focuses on strengthening nonprofit organizations through better systems, better information, and better decision infrastructure.

Warren has spent twenty years working inside charitable, civic, and mission-driven organizations, including organizations with complex funding, governance, and operating structures. His career has included work with a foundation with a national footprint, a statewide nonprofit with a programmatic presence in every jurisdiction, and a membership association with a national presence.

That experience shaped his belief that mission-driven organizations deserve the same level of operational, quantitative, and technical sophistication often reserved for larger corporate or technology-centered environments. His work draws from operations management, strategic management, business intelligence, data analytics, and applied information systems to help organizations solve business problems they often lack the capacity, infrastructure, or technical staff to address.

His professional work has included standing up business intelligence functions, improving board governance systems, supporting executive operations, strengthening procurement practices, improving development and finance workflows, and helping translate organizational values into operational policy. He is especially interested in how institutions actually function: how information moves, how decisions are made, how resources are allocated, and how people experience the systems around them.

His current interests include disrupting the nonprofit starvation cycle, applying artificial intelligence and machine learning to operational decision-making, and expanding access to technical fields for people who want to use technology outside of traditional technology-sector roles. He is especially interested in the space where nonprofit management, data science, governance, and public systems overlap.

Professional background

Warren’s professional background spans nonprofit operations, board governance, executive support, business intelligence, procurement, reporting systems, and organizational administration. Across these settings, he has worked on a recurring problem from different angles: how organizations can become clearer, more disciplined, more equitable, and more capable of making decisions with the information they already have.

His work often starts with the practical realities of organizational life: scattered records, complex workflows, informal knowledge, limited capacity, and decisions that have to be made without perfect information. He is interested in building systems that make work easier to understand, easier to manage, and easier to improve.

Applied interests

Warren’s applied interests are rooted in the nonprofit sector but extend into analytics, information systems, technical capacity-building, and organizational design. He is interested in how nonprofits can use business intelligence, machine learning, dashboards, workflow documentation, and data infrastructure without losing sight of mission, equity, or human context.

Much of his work comes back to a practical belief: better systems give people more room to make better decisions. That does not mean reducing organizations to metrics. It means designing information flows, tools, and routines that help people see what is happening, understand what matters, and act with more confidence.

Research and doctoral interests

Warren is preparing for eventual doctoral-level study focused on nonprofit organizational sustainability, governance, analytics capacity, and operational infrastructure. His research interests center on how nonprofit organizations invest in, manage, and sometimes under-resource the systems that make mission delivery possible.

He is especially interested in how knowledge management, data infrastructure, workflow design, and information systems can help nonprofit leaders move from reactive administration toward stronger, evidence-informed operating models. His work is applied in orientation: the goal is not only to study organizational problems, but to develop practical approaches that can improve decision-making and strengthen mission impact.

Black cultural and institutional interests

Jones’ intellectual and cultural interests are deeply shaped by Black art, Black authorship, and the institutional legacy of historically Black colleges and universities. He is interested in HBCUs not only as important educational institutions, but as sites of intellectual production, professional formation, cultural memory, and advanced study.

Selected cultural and intellectual influences include Gordon Parks, W. E. B. Du Bois, Maya Angelou, Toni Morrison, James Baldwin, Percival Everett, Jon Batiste, Kehinde Wiley, Ta-Nehisi Coates, and Ava DuVernay. Their work reflects the range of Black cultural production that informs his interests in art, memory, language, institutions, public life, and self-definition.

Travel, cities, and museums

Outside of his professional life, Warren is drawn to experiences that help people see the world from another angle, whether through music, literature, art, food, travel, or conversation with people whose lives and assumptions differ from his own. His travels have taken him across North America, the Caribbean, and Europe, including Canada, Mexico, Bermuda, St. Lucia, The Bahamas, Barbados, Sint Maarten, Spain, the Netherlands, Germany, England, Portugal, and France.

His favorite international city is Amsterdam. His favorite U.S. city is New Orleans, a city he values for its culture, food, music, atmosphere, and sense of place. Museums that have made a particular impression include the Musée des Arts et Métiers in Paris, STRAAT Museum in Amsterdam, Hirshhorn Museum in Washington, DC, Urban Nation Museum in Berlin, and the Mütter Museum in Philadelphia.

In 2026, he is scheduled to visit Montréal for the first time. Future travel interests include Johannesburg, Accra, Dakar, Cape Town, Nairobi, Hong Kong, Salvador, Cartagena, and El Nido.

Purpose of this site

Warren created this site as a public archive for selected projects, working papers, practitioner writing, research notes, and professional reflections. It is meant to function less like a traditional portfolio landing page and more like a working knowledge base: a place to organize what he has built, what he is studying, and what he is thinking through.

The site will continue to develop over time, with dedicated pages for selected projects, working papers, practitioner writing, and themes related to nonprofit operations, governance, data analytics, artificial intelligence, public systems, and organizational life.
