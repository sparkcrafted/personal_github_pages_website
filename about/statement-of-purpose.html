Statement

My research interests center on the ways nonprofit organizations use investments in knowledge management infrastructure to improve business operations. I am especially interested in the ways nonprofit organizations can optimize business functions and enhance service delivery using data, information systems, analytics, and workflow management. My hope is that my work and research can help organizational leaders move from seeing data infrastructure and knowledge management activities as an administrative luxury to viewing them as essential infrastructure critical to an impactful mission and organizational sustainability.

As a practitioner, I have committed my 20-year professional career to working with charitable and civic organizations to execute and enhance administration and business operations. This work of nonprofit organizations has always been important to me. This is, in part, because I have seen and experienced the many ways their outcomes help people and improve communities. In my professional experience, nonprofits are very good at crafting impactful mission statements, attracting devoted staff to do the work, and engaging donors who care about their outcomes, but they usually proudly operate with razor-thin overhead margins. This model leaves few resources to support important operational functions, including technology systems. These experiences have helped shape my point of view that nonprofit organizations deserve the same managerial savvy, technical expertise, and business intelligence precision as organizations in any other sector.

As a professional, I intentionally choose to work for nonprofit organizations that are statewide or national in scope. My appreciation for nonprofits operating at scale is tied to my interest in organizational complexity, which requires strategic operations, strong systems, and clear workflows. In my view, enhancing these systems can strengthen an organization’s impact and improve service delivery for the communities in which they operate. My academic training and professional experience have led me to pursue a practitioner-scholar approach that allows me to use the challenges I encounter in my professional practice as a source of researchable business problems where I can leverage applied research to improve how nonprofits use resources, make decisions, and optimize impact.

I am seeking doctoral-level training to complete the final layer in a strategic three-layer professional skill stack while developing subject matter expertise in a research area about which I am passionate. My B.S. in Management Studies and M.B.A. training provided me with business fluency. These are the learning experiences I use when performing as a senior leader within organizations and in my consultancy practice while advising nonprofit executives and boards of directors. My M.S. in Data Analytics and Visualization training teaches me quantitative and technical skills, as well as scientific-method preparation. This exposure sparked my interest in using applied management science to improve operational precision in the nonprofit sector.

I view Doctor of Business Administration training as the pièce de résistance in my skill stack. Having the opportunity to combine my previous training with the applied research skills that would allow me to test business questions with doctoral-level rigor and produce scholarly writing that other practitioners can use to solve similar problems within their organizations is significant to me. I believe strongly that the nonprofit sector deserves professionals with these types of capabilities. On a personal level, I learned from family members at an early age to view education as a social and economic equalizer. I view my pursuit of doctoral study as both a logical extension of my career path and reflective of values I learned during my formative years.

My decision to pursue a Doctor of Business Administration program was influenced by the type of doctoral training I am seeking. I wanted a program that emphasized quantitative methods, analytics, and applied research in both the coursework and dissertation process, and I wanted to study in a business school environment with a serious and respected research culture. I believe this doctoral training will strengthen my consulting practice, enable me to produce applied research for the nonprofit and public sectors, and prepare me to eventually teach in a business school.

I do not see the D.B.A. as simply a credential. I see the program as preparation for a practitioner-scholar career that helps human services organizations build the systems and operational capacity needed to serve people well. I believe doctoral study can provide a proving ground where I can hone and advance the quantitative analysis and applied data science skills I developed during my master’s training while developing subject matter expertise in nonprofit operations that can support the sector.

Research interests

The statement reflects Warren’s broader research interest in nonprofit operational capacity, knowledge management infrastructure, data-informed decision-making, governance systems, and the use of applied analytics in mission-driven organizations. These interests are also developed in his Applied Research Statement and selected Working Papers.

Professional foundation

The statement also reflects Warren’s professional background in nonprofit administration, governance operations, business intelligence, executive operations, workflow improvement, and organizational systems. His doctoral interests are grounded in problems he has observed directly through professional practice and are connected to his broader professional experience.

Doctoral purpose

Warren’s interest in doctoral study is tied to a practitioner-scholar identity: using applied research to study real business problems, develop rigorous insights, and produce work that can help nonprofit and public-sector leaders strengthen organizational systems. This purpose also connects to his academic journey and long-term interest in contributing to business education, nonprofit practice, and applied organizational research.
