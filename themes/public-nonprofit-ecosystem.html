Core question How operating systems shape mission delivery

Mission delivery depends on more than individual organizations. Nonprofits operate inside a larger ecosystem of public agencies, philanthropy, regulation, funding streams, community institutions, infrastructure, and governance relationships. I am interested in how that ecosystem shapes what organizations are actually able to do.

Organizations inside systems

Nonprofits are often evaluated as though their outcomes are produced entirely within the boundaries of a single organization. In practice, mission delivery depends on relationships with government, funders, boards, vendors, community partners, regulatory systems, and other institutions. Operational performance is therefore partly an ecosystem question.

Capacity and the starvation cycle

I am particularly interested in the nonprofit starvation cycle: the tendency to underinvest in administrative, technological, analytical, and operational infrastructure while expecting increasingly sophisticated results. Strong programs depend on finance, data, governance, technology, procurement, human resources, and management systems that are often treated as secondary to mission rather than essential to it.

Public infrastructure and opportunity

Public institutions and infrastructure shape access to opportunity. Transportation, education, digital systems, administrative processes, and civic institutions all influence how people encounter services and how organizations deliver them. This makes public-system design relevant to organizational performance, equity, and community outcomes.

A practitioner-scholar lens

My interest in this ecosystem grows out of nearly two decades of work in mission-driven organizations. I approach these questions as both a practitioner and an applied researcher: examining how institutions actually function, where capacity breaks down, and how data, systems, governance, and management can strengthen the infrastructure behind mission delivery.
