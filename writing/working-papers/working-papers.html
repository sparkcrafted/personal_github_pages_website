Working papers are research-oriented manuscripts that are in development, under revision, or being prepared for broader circulation. They allow ideas to be shared while still making clear that the work may continue to evolve.

Warren uses this section to organize selected scholarly and practitioner-oriented papers connected to nonprofit organizations, governance systems, knowledge management, data infrastructure, leadership representation, and organizational capacity.

These papers reflect an applied research orientation. They begin with problems encountered in practice and develop those problems into structured arguments, frameworks, and questions that can contribute to management, nonprofit operations, and organizational decision-making.

Overview

Jones’ current working papers examine how mission-driven organizations build, maintain, and sometimes neglect the internal systems that shape their ability to operate effectively. The papers are connected by a shared interest in the infrastructure beneath organizational performance: knowledge systems, governance routines, leadership structures, information flows, and decision processes. His work is grounded in nonprofit and mission-driven contexts, but his questions extend into broader management concerns: how organizations learn, how leaders make decisions, how internal capacity is built, and how operational systems influence long-term sustainability.

Current working papers

Knowledge Management as Operational Infrastructure: Information Systems and Organizational Capacity in Nonprofit Organizations

Jones, W. A. · Working paper · 2025

This paper examines knowledge management as a form of operational infrastructure in nonprofit organizations. It considers how information systems, documentation practices, staff knowledge, data access, and organizational memory shape nonprofit capacity and continuity.

The paper is especially concerned with organizations that depend heavily on informal knowledge: the long-tenured employee who knows where everything is, the spreadsheet only one person understands, and the process that works because someone remembers how it has always been done. It argues that knowledge management should be treated not as an administrative extra, but as core infrastructure for mission performance and organizational sustainability.

Who Leads Nonprofit Organizations? Leadership Representation, Governance, and Community Alignment in the Nonprofit Sector

Jones, W. A. · Working paper · 2026

This paper examines nonprofit leadership representation, governance, and community alignment. It considers who leads nonprofit organizations, how leadership demographics and governance structures relate to the communities organizations serve, and why representation matters for legitimacy, accountability, decision-making, and mission alignment.

The paper is grounded in a practical concern: nonprofit organizations often exist to serve communities that may not be fully represented in executive leadership, board leadership, or governance decision-making. The paper explores the organizational and governance implications of that gap.

Research themes

The current working papers are connected by several recurring themes:

  • Operational capacity: How nonprofit organizations build the internal systems needed to sustain mission work.
  • Knowledge infrastructure: How organizations capture, organize, and transfer information that would otherwise remain informal or person-dependent.
  • Governance and decision-making: How boards, executives, and leadership teams use information, representation, and structure to make decisions.
  • Community alignment: How organizational leadership and governance reflect, or fail to reflect, the communities an organization exists to serve.
  • Applied research: How problems first encountered in practice can be developed into research questions, frameworks, and tools for broader use.

Status note

The papers listed here are working papers. They may be revised, expanded, reorganized, or replaced as the underlying research develops. Their inclusion on this site is intended to document active areas of inquiry and provide a public record of Warren’s developing research interests.

Working paper status does not mean the ideas are casual or informal. It means the manuscripts are part of an active research and writing process.
